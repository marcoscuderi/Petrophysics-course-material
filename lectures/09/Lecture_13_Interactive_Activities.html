Explanation

Resolved shear is zero for flaws parallel or normal to σ₁ and reaches its maximum at 45°. Friction also depends on normal traction, so the easiest sliding orientation lies below 45° when μ > 0. For the current μ, the analytical optimum is shown on the Mohr circle.

Expected: students separate “largest shear traction” from “first frictional activation.”

Explanation

Confinement works twice. It raises normal traction and therefore friction on the parent flaw. It also contributes a closing term on the wing crack. Once a wing has nucleated, the wedging influence weakens as the tip moves away, while clamping becomes more important, so an isolated wing tends to grow stably and arrest.

Instructor notes

Suggested comparison: σ₃ = 10 MPa versus 80 MPa with all other controls fixed.

Emphasis: crack initiation does not imply runaway failure. Interaction is still missing.

Explanation

Without interaction, many isolated cracks can accumulate while the specimen continues to harden. Interaction changes the local driving force, promotes linkage and concentrates damage into a connected band. Localization depends on spatial organization and connectivity, not only on the total number of cracks.

Instructor notes

Run twice: first with interaction off, then reset and repeat with interaction on.

Ask: which measurement detects localization better: mean crack density, the largest cluster, or path-dependent velocity?
